# Product Composition and Panel Gravity Benchmarks for Uzbekistan's Exports to Central Asian Neighbors

**Author:** Javohir Mirzakahhorov (Shams Private School, 11th grade) · javoxirmirzaqahhorov@gmail.com

📄 **[Read the full paper (PDF)](./Uzbekistan_Central_Asia_Exports_Mirzakahhorov.pdf)**

## Abstract

This paper examines changes in Uzbekistan's merchandise exports to Kazakhstan, the Kyrgyz Republic, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan, and tests how strongly a gravity-based comparison depends on model design. Bilateral six-digit CEPII BACI data for 2010–2024 are combined with World Development Indicators GDP and CEPII GeoDist variables.

The four destinations' share of Uzbekistan's exports fell from 15.99% in 2022 to 11.44% in 2023 and 9.92% in 2024. In 2023, however, their export value rose by US$77.44 million; the share declined because national exports grew faster. Their export value then fell by US$526.93 million in 2024. HS61 knitted apparel, HS87 vehicles and parts, and HS31 fertilizers account for US$322.25 million of that decline in net terms.

Panel Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) models fitted after withholding all Uzbekistan-to-neighbor observations give 2024 observed-to-predicted ratios of 4.98 and 2.98 in two conventional specifications. Adding former-Soviet and joint-landlocked pair indicators changes those ratios to 1.03 and 0.57. Results are similar when the comparison set expands from 83 to 160 economies, and a holdout exercise on other contiguous former-Soviet pairs finds broad prediction errors. Export-share accounting and product decomposition give robust descriptive results, whereas the sign and size of the gravity gap do not support conclusions about trade-policy effects or unrealized export capacity.

## Key findings

- **2023:** the neighbors' share fell even though exports to them rose, because total exports (boosted by gold) grew 44%. The share excluding gold also fell, so gold doesn't explain all of it.
- **2024:** a real decline of US$526.93 million (−18.85%), concentrated in different product groups in each destination.
- **Gravity models:** benchmark results change several-fold depending on which historical and geographic pair characteristics are included, so a single "over/under-trading" ratio isn't a reliable policy indicator.

## Data

- **Trade:** CEPII BACI, HS 1992 classification, release V202601 (2010–2024)
- **GDP:** World Bank World Development Indicators (current US$)
- **Geography:** CEPII GeoDist (distance, contiguity, common language, colonial ties, landlocked status)
